Plan: Body Paragraph Bifurcation

Divide the categories logically to write two balanced body paragraphs.

Group your data
Body Paragraph 1
Covers Stages

Flour Silo Storage ➔ Dough Sheets ➔ Slicing Dough Strips

Key Features to Describe
  • Initially, raw flour is transported from storage silos to a mixing machine, where it is homogenized with water and oil.
  • Subsequently, the mixture is passed through a series of rollers to flatten it into thin dough sheets.
  • Following this, these flat sheets are sheared by machinery to form wavy dough strips.
Body Paragraph 2
Covers Stages

Forming Noodle Discs ➔ Frying & Dehydrating ➔ Cup Packaging ➔ Sealing

Key Features to Describe
  • Thereafter, the wavy strips are portioned and shaped into circular noodle discs.
  • These discs are then cooked in hot oil, which parboils them, and are subsequently dried to remove moisture.
  • Next, the dehydrated noodle discs are dispensed into cups, followed by the addition of dried vegetables and spices.
  • Eventually, the cups are hermetically sealed and labelled, completing the production line.

SAMPLE CONCLUSION

In conclusion, introducing practical and creative skills in secondary schools presents certain challenges, such as curriculum overload and increased funding requirements. However, these drawbacks are significantly overshadowed by the benefits of fostering self-reliance, enhancing motor skills, and offering alternative career paths. Therefore, schools should strive to incorporate these vocational classes into their regular schedules to ensure a more holistic education.

Plan: Body Paragraph 1

Focus: Advantages of Teaching Non-Academic Skills

Supporting Points & Examples
1. Fosters Self-Reliance

Practical skills like cooking and basic repairs prepare students to manage daily life effectively without depending on others.

Example: Students who learn culinary basics can prepare nutritious meals independently, reducing reliance on unhealthy fast food.
2. Cultivates Creative & Motor Skills

Hands-on subjects like drawing and woodwork develop fine motor coordination and spatial reasoning.

Example: Designing and assembling wooden projects requires precise spatial awareness, translating to improved hand-eye coordination.
3. Unlocks Vocational Career Paths

Early exposure to vocational arts helps students identify talents that could lead to rewarding non-academic professions.

Example: A student discovering a talent for woodworking might pursue highly skilled trades like carpentry or industrial design.
4. Offers Cognitive Relief

Practical and creative classes provide a mental break from highly demanding core academic subjects, reducing student stress.

Example: Engaging in drawing sessions helps students express emotions and relax, improving overall focus in mathematics and science.

Plan: Body Paragraph 2

Focus: Disadvantages of Teaching Non-Academic Skills

Supporting Points & Examples
1. Dilutes Academic Focus

Allocating time to practical arts reduces the hours available for critical subjects like science, mathematics, and literacy.

Example: Shifting classroom hours to woodworking might leave students less prepared for competitive university entrance exams.
2. Places Financial Strain on Schools

Equipping workshops and kitchens requires substantial funding for tools, safety gear, and specialized staff.

Example: Purchasing sewing machines, stoves, and carpentry benches can deplete a public school's limited annual budget.
3. Introduces Safety & Liability Risks

Working with sharp tools, stoves, and heavy equipment increases the likelihood of student injuries in class.

Example: Woodwork rooms require strict safety protocols to prevent accidents involving power tools or sharp hand implements.
4. Reinforces Socio-economic Tracking

Directing students towards practical skills classes can sometimes divert underprivileged students away from academic pursuits.

Example: Schools in poorer districts might over-emphasize vocational training, limiting students' access to higher education.
