2. Sample Overview
"Overall, eating fast food once a week and once or twice a month accounted for the largest proportions throughout the period, whereas daily consumption and never eating fast food consistently represented the smallest shares. Plan: Body Paragraph Bifurcation

Divide the categories logically to write two balanced body paragraphs.

Group your data
Body Paragraph 1
Covers Frequencies

Every day • Several times a week • Once a week

Key Features to Describe
  • Daily consumption was the least common habit (~3% to 4%)
  • Several times a week visits rose slightly in 2006 before declining in 2013
  • Once a week was the peak frequency in 2003 and 2006, declining by 2013
  • Frequent eating patterns showed a minor downward trend overall
Body Paragraph 2
Covers Frequencies

Once or twice a month • A few times a year • Never

Key Features to Describe
  • Once or twice a month visits rose, becoming the largest category in 2013 (~33%)
  • A few times a year visits remained stable at around 15%
  • Never eating fast food consistently accounted for a small minority (under 5%)
  • Occasional eating patterns became slightly more common than frequent visits

Paragraph 1 Flow (Frequent Consumption)
  • Daily consumption: Remained the least common habit, with only a very small proportion of people eating fast food every day throughout the period.
  • Several times a week: The percentage of people eating fast food several times a week rose slightly in 2006 before declining again in 2013.
  • Once a week: Once a week was the most common frequency in both 2003 and 2006, although its proportion declined by the end of the period.
Paragraph 2 Flow (Occasional Consumption)
  • Once or twice a month: The proportion of people eating fast food once or twice a month increased overall and became the largest category in 2013.
  • A few times a year: The percentage of individuals visiting fast-food restaurants a few times a year remained relatively stable throughout the period.
  • Never: Likewise, those who never consumed fast food consistently accounted for only a small minority.

Workbook Power Expressions

Learn the essential "Power 16" workbook expressions categorised for Band 9 comparative and trend writing.

Workbook Top 16
Tier 1: Essential Verbs
  • • Accounted for: Represented a particular share (e.g. Monthly visits accounted for the largest share.)
  • • Represented: (e.g. Daily visits represented the smallest proportion.)
  • • Overtook: Surpassed another category (e.g. Monthly visits overtook weekly visits in 2013.)
  • • Witnessed: Experienced a change (e.g. The decade witnessed a notable shift in occasional dining.)
Tier 2: Trends & Comparisons
  • • Rose/Declined steadily: (e.g. The weekly proportion declined steadily over the decade.)
  • • Remained stable / Levelled off: (e.g. Annual visits remained stable across the three years.)
  • • Likewise / Correspondingly: (e.g. Likewise, the proportion of non-consumers remained low.)
  • • In contrast / By comparison: (e.g. In contrast, daily consumption remained marginal.)
Tier 3: Band 9 Finishers
  • • A comparable pattern: (e.g. A comparable pattern was observed in 2006.)
  • • The reverse was true: (e.g. The reverse was true for daily consumption.)
  • • Mirroring this trend: (e.g. Mirroring this trend, monthly visits also increased.)
  • • Particularly evident: (e.g. This trend was particularly evident in the final year.)

SAMPLE INTRODUCTION

Nowadays, considerably fewer people devote time to hobbies than in the past due to increasing professional commitments, digital distractions, and the growing pressure to remain competitive. This trend has significant consequences for individual well-being, creativity, social cohesion, and overall quality of life.

SAMPLE CONCLUSION

In conclusion, increasing work demands, digital distractions, and competitive lifestyles have significantly reduced people's participation in hobbies. As a result, this trend negatively affects mental well-being, creativity, social relationships, and overall life satisfaction. Therefore, individuals should consciously devote time to recreational pursuits in order to maintain a healthier and more balanced lifestyle.

Plan: Body Paragraph 1

Focus: Investing in New Infrastructure

Supporting Points & Examples
1. Accommodates Population Growth

Modern facilities can meet the demands of expanding urban populations.

Example: New housing, transport networks and hospitals can ease pressure on overcrowded cities.
2. Enhances Economic Productivity

Efficient infrastructure facilitates business activity and improves connectivity.

Example: Upgraded roads and public transport can reduce commuting and freight-delivery times.
3. Improves Public Safety

New structures can incorporate contemporary safety and environmental standards.

Example: Modern bridges and buildings are designed to withstand structural and climatic risks more effectively.
4. Supports Technological Progress

Contemporary infrastructure can accommodate emerging technologies and changing public needs.

Example: Smart transport systems and energy-efficient buildings can improve urban efficiency.

Plan: Body Paragraph 2

Focus: Preserving Old Buildings

Supporting Points & Examples
1. Protects Cultural Heritage

Historic structures embody a society's architectural and cultural legacy.

Example: Preserving landmarks allows future generations to retain a tangible connection with their history.
2. Strengthens Tourism

Heritage sites can attract visitors and generate local economic activity.

Example: Historic districts often support museums, hotels, restaurants and local businesses.
3. Maintains Architectural Identity

Older buildings give cities a distinctive character that modern developments may lack.

Example: Traditional architecture can differentiate one city from increasingly uniform urban landscapes.
4. Encourages Sustainable Development

Reusing existing structures can reduce demolition waste and conserve construction resources.

Example: Converting historic buildings into offices or cultural centres can extend their useful life.
